# Innovation Feature Rationale

RecruitO's proposed innovation is a recruitment workflow assistant that connects job drafting with external job sharing and candidate intake. Many small teams move between separate documents, job boards, inboxes, and spreadsheets. That handoff creates repeated entry and makes it easy for the public job post, application form, and internal evaluation criteria to drift apart.

The hiring manager describes a role in a conversational interface. RecruitO extracts the details the manager actually supplied, asks about missing information, and hands a complete brief to the existing job-description workflow. That workflow retrieves policy material, drafts the description, extracts structured requirements, checks the result, and stores a draft for human review. The manager remains responsible for editing and approving the job. RecruitO does not publish an unreviewed draft.

After approval, the manager can send the post through a bundled MCP service to Blogger's Mail2Blogger address. The external post links back to RecruitO's application form, while the public jobs view shows a compact job card. This gives the prototype one end-to-end path from role brief to candidate application without requiring a paid job-board API. Mail2Blogger has practical limits: it requires a private Blogger address and SMTP credentials, it creates a post by email, and RecruitO cannot reliably obtain the exact resulting post URL. The integration is therefore a demonstration route, not a substitute for a commercial applicant-tracking or job-distribution network.

The assistant is deliberately role scoped. Candidates can ask about their own application and published job cards. Interviewers can ask about assigned interviews and their transcripts. Hiring managers can ask about jobs, scores, rankings, and interview schedules. Each role receives a separate server-side tool allowlist, and the current user identity comes from the authenticated session. The assistant cannot change permissions because a user asks it to, and output does not replace human hiring judgment.

This feature is useful because it joins several small workflow steps while preserving approval, evidence, and access boundaries. Its value should be demonstrated with synthetic job and candidate data. Before any public or real-candidate use, the team must validate the Blogger email path, model behavior, privacy disclosures, and trainer approval of the revised feature scope.
